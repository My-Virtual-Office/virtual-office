\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}

The shift toward remote and hybrid work has scattered the modern workplace across
a dozen disconnected tools: one application for video meetings, another for
chat, others for tasks, calendars, and file sharing. This fragmentation erodes
the two qualities that make a physical office productive --- \emph{presence}
(knowing who is around and available) and \emph{spontaneity} (the ability to walk
up to a colleague and start talking). \voname{} is a graduation project that
addresses this gap by delivering a single, spatial, all-in-one collaboration
platform: a digital headquarters where teams appear as avatars in an animated
2D office and where messaging, meetings, tasks, scheduling, and workspace
administration live under one roof.

The platform is engineered as three coordinated front-ends over a
distributed backend. An immersive \textbf{2D virtual office} --- built with the
Phaser game engine on the client and an authoritative Colyseus multiplayer
server --- renders avatars that walk around a tile-mapped office, sit at desks,
gather at whiteboards, and start proximity-based voice, video, and screen-sharing
sessions the moment they move close to one another. A \textbf{React web client}
provides a full Slack-style collaboration surface --- workspaces, channels,
direct messages, threads, room calls (powered by Agora real-time media), and a
live notification centre. A \textbf{Flutter mobile client} brings the messaging
experience to phones, complete with optimistic delivery, read receipts, threads,
and cross-state push notifications. Underneath, a \textbf{microservices backend}
of nine cooperating Spring~Boot services --- gateway, user, workspace, room,
chat, tasks, calendar, notifications, and a shared library --- handles identity,
spatial state, real-time messaging, and event-driven side effects, coordinated
through an Nginx gateway, per-service databases (PostgreSQL, MongoDB, Redis),
and a RabbitMQ message bus.

This book documents the complete engineering of the system: the problem it
solves and the requirements it targets; the microservices architecture and the
cross-cutting patterns that hold it together (token-based WebSocket
authentication, the \code{\{action, payload\}} real-time envelope, event-driven
service decoupling, and store-the-foreign-key service boundaries); the internal
design of each backend service, including its data model, REST and WebSocket
APIs, and message flows; the real-time multiplayer model of the 2D office; and
the implementation of the web and mobile clients. It closes with the project's
testing approach, an honest evaluation of what was achieved, and a roadmap for
future work.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} virtual office, spatial collaboration, microservices,
Spring Boot, real-time systems, WebSocket, STOMP, WebRTC, Phaser, Colyseus,
React, Flutter, event-driven architecture.
