02 · Motivation

Remote work broke the office into a dozen tabs

The shift to remote & hybrid work scattered the workplace across disconnected tools — one app for meetings, another for chat, others for tasks, calendars, and files.

  • This fragmentation erodes the two things a physical office gives a team for free:
  • Presence — knowing who is around and available right now.Status dots in five apps don't tell you the room is warm.
  • Spontaneity — the ability to walk up to a colleague and just start talking.Scheduling a call to ask a 20-second question kills the conversation.
  • Existing tools (Slack, Zoom, Gather) each solve a slice — none give a team a single, shared place.

06 · Architecture

The gateway is the single front door

A microservices style, chosen so each concern — identity, spatial state, messaging, media — evolves and scales independently behind one edge.

  • Edge-validated identity. The gateway verifies the JWT once, then injects trusted headers — X-User-Id X-User-Role X-User-Email — that every service downstream trusts.Authentication logic removed from five services at a stroke; the edge strips any spoofable headers.
  • Per-service databases. No shared schema — services own their data and cross reference by stored foreign keys.
  • Internal API lets server-to-server calls (e.g. the office server → workspace) bypass the public edge, guarded by an X-Internal-Token.

07 · The Patterns That Hold It Together

Four decisions, applied everywhere

Real-time auth

Ticket-based WebSocket handshake

Sockets can't carry auth headers — so clients exchange a short-lived Redis ticket for a connection. One recipe reused by every client.

Message shape

The {action, payload} envelope

Every real-time message shares one uniform shape, so each client needs only a single dispatch loop.

Service decoupling

Event-driven side effects

Services emit events onto RabbitMQ instead of calling each other; delivery is idempotent, so retries never duplicate.

Boundaries

Polyglot persistence

Relational integrity for workspaces, documents for messages, Redis for ephemeral presence & tickets — the store fits the data.

09 · How Services Talk

Synchronous when you must, asynchronous when you can

Synchronous · internal REST

  • Direct lookups & validations behind the edge.e.g. office server asks workspace for a member's desk & status.
  • Guarded by the internal token, blocked at the gateway.

Asynchronous · RabbitMQ bus

  • Side effects flow as NotificationEvents.Survives outages; buys resilience at the cost of eventual consistency.
  • Idempotent delivery — retries never double-fire.
  • Polyglot persistence: MySQL users · PostgreSQL workspace · MongoDB messages · Redis presence & tickets.

10 · The 2D Virtual Office · SkyOffice

A live world, not a video grid

Built on the Phaser game engine with an authoritative Colyseus multiplayer server — the server owns the truth, the client predicts and interpolates.

  • Avatars walk a tile-mapped office across multiple floors, sit at per-person desks, and gather at whiteboards.
  • Client-side prediction keeps your own movement instant; remote interpolation smooths everyone else.
  • Server-authoritative interactive objects — computers & whiteboards are claimed and released consistently for all players.
  • A React + Redux overlay renders the UI on top of the game canvas.

11 · Proximity Communication

Get close, and the conversation starts

The defining interaction: move your avatar near someone and voice, video & screen-share open automatically — the online version of walking over to a desk.

  • The room service computes voice groups from avatar positions — who can hear whom is a function of where you stand.
  • It mints short-lived media tokens but never carries the media — audio/video flow peer-to-peer.
  • Media transport uses WebRTC (office client) and Agora (web room calls).
  • Walk away, and the call quietly ends — no hang-up, no scheduling.

18 · Lessons Learned

What the engineering taught us

Consistency

Uniform patterns pay compound dividends

One ticket handshake + one envelope meant each new client needed only one connection recipe and one dispatch loop.

Trade-offs

Async decoupling buys resilience, costs immediacy

Event-driven binding survives outages — but clients must be designed to tolerate eventual consistency.

Security

Security is easiest in one place

Validate identity once at the edge and have every service trust it — provided the edge strips spoofable headers.

Data

Fit the store to the data

Relational, document & in-memory stores each simplified the service that used them.

19 · Future Work

Breadth & hardening — not a change of direction

  • Complete tasks & calendar — the largest functional gaps; notifications already consume their events.
  • Fully integrate the office live — boot rooms from workspace config, stream positions to the proximity engine in steady state.
  • Broaden mobile beyond chat — workspaces, rooms, a real JWT login.
  • Harden for production — secret manager, platform-level RBAC, server-side authorisation.
  • Reliability & observability — dead-letter queues, presence reaper, distributed tracing & alerting.

20 · Conclusion

We rebuilt what an office gives for free

Avatars share a live space, conversations begin by proximity, and persistent messaging, rooms, workspaces & notifications surround that space — across a web, a mobile, and a game client over a coherent microservices backend.

  • It stands as a demonstration, and as a foundation ready to be carried further.
